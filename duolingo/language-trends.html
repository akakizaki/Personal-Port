Overview

An interactive data story about how the world learns language

In my Intro to Info Visualization course, I collaborated with classmates Huy Le, Timothy Nguyen, and Michael Vega to implement a web-based interactive data story using D3.js, focusing on global language learning trends in recent years.

This project examines the impacts of technology, socioeconomic factors, demographics, and migration on language learning through an ethnographic lens. Our analysis involved reviewing data from governments, language platforms, and polling sources, coupled with exploratory data analysis, to identify patterns in language learning popularity and investigate anomalies like heightened monolingualism in certain U.S. regions.

The findings are presented through a web-based interactive narrative featuring five dynamic visualizations created with D3.js. The goal is to highlight significant factors influencing language acquisition and educate readers on how societal and technological factors shape language learning trends.

Key Question

How do technology platforms, socioeconomic factors, and migration patterns shape what languages people learn — and which ones disappear?

01
Platforms reshaping dominance

The dominance of English on the internet has declined as other languages gain presence online via platforms like Duolingo and Babbel.

02
Geography over culture

Foreign-language speakers in each U.S. state correlate with ESL enrollment, suggesting globalization influences language learning more than regional origin.

03
Digital flexibility wins

Platforms like Duolingo reflect increasing interest in learning languages — self-paced, flexible, and engaging over rigid classroom formats.

Process

How did we do it?

In our initial planning phase, we began with an idea brainstorming session where we listed potential data sources. The primary data source we ultimately used was the Duolingo Annual Language Report. During the brainstorming process, we collected relevant data, conducted exploratory analysis using Tableau, Excel, Python, and R, and created preliminary visualizations to investigate our proposed questions.

Next, we developed a story arc by identifying a main message based on the insights we uncovered. We selected one key insight we felt would resonate most with our audience and formulated it into a concise message ("so what") in one sentence.

01
Data collection & exploration

Sourced data from government databases, Duolingo Annual Reports, and polling sources. Used Python and R for cleaning, analysis, and identifying key patterns.

02
Story arc & storyboarding

Each team member individually sketched potential visualizations to support the story. We collectively reviewed and voted on the best designs for implementation.

03
Interactive implementation

Built five dynamic D3.js visualizations embedded in a React-based scrollytelling narrative. Linked visualizations allowed users to explore connections across datasets.

Design Evolution

What changed after user feedback

To refine our implementation and enhance the delivery of our data story, we conducted a Think-Aloud study to gather user feedback. Participants were asked to interact with the prototype while verbalizing their thoughts.

✓ Clarity of key message

Most testers found the intended key message clear and appreciated how the visualizations communicated global language-learning trends. However, some raised questions about contextualization.

✓ Interactive elements

Testers found the interactive features of the bar graph and linked visualizations engaging and intuitive — allowing deeper exploration of the data.

⚠ Call to action

Testers suggested the call to action could be more prominently emphasized by including a dedicated key takeaway or summary section within the visualization.

⚠ Context gap

Certain messages were not immediately clear just by examining the graphs — users needed more narrative scaffolding around the data.

Reflection

What did I learn?

This project was both an educational and enjoyable experience, but it was far from easy. It pushed me out of my comfort zone and challenged me to apply what I had learned in information visualization and D3.js to a real-world problem — from collecting and cleaning data to designing effective visualizations and executing our storyboard.

One of the biggest challenges was ensuring our visualizations effectively communicated the story we wanted to tell. It wasn't enough to make the graphs look good — they had to be intuitive, informative, and aligned with the key message. This required multiple iterations and feedback sessions, which taught me the importance of embracing constructive criticism and remaining flexible.

Another significant aspect was learning to collaborate effectively in a team environment. Coordination wasn't always straightforward, as we all had different ideas, skill sets, and responsibilities. However, this diversity became our strength as we learned to listen to each other, compromise, and combine our efforts.
